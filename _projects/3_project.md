## Research Overview

This research investigates whether quantum mechanics can be derived from information-theoretic or operational principles, focusing on the role of observers, entanglement, and quantum correlations in defining physical reality. The work explores links between quantum information, measurement theory, and emergent spacetime concepts.

## Research Objectives

### Information-Theoretic Approach to Quantum Mechanics
- Deriving quantum mechanical principles from information processing constraints
- Operational foundations of quantum theory
- Information causality and its implications

### Role of Observers and Measurement
- Quantum measurement theory and observer effects
- Decoherence and the emergence of classical reality
- Relational interpretations of quantum mechanics

### Entanglement and Quantum Correlations
- Entanglement as a fundamental resource
- Quantum vs. classical correlations
- Non-local effects and their implications

### Emergent Spacetime Concepts
- Spacetime emergence from quantum information
- Holographic principles in quantum gravity
- Quantum information geometry

## Theoretical Framework

### Information-Theoretic Constraints
- Principles of information processing
- Computational complexity bounds
- Thermodynamic considerations

### Operational Quantum Theory
- Preparation and measurement procedures
- Transformation protocols
- State discrimination tasks

## Methodological Approach

### Mathematical Formalism
- Quantum information measures (entropy, mutual information)
- Process tomography and quantum state estimation
- Category theory and quantum logic

### Computational Methods
- Quantum circuit simulations
- Numerical optimization techniques
- Statistical analysis of quantum correlations

## Expected Contributions

### Theoretical Insights
- New perspectives on quantum foundations
