Biography

I was born and raised in a small village, Madhuban, Kapilvastu, Nepal, in a humble family consisting of my elder brother, mother, and father. My parents, despite their immense desire for education, never had the opportunity to attend school. They saw their dreams reflected in me and found happiness in watching their unfulfilled aspirations slowly being realized through my achievements.

Their hard work and sacrifices have been the foundation of my journey toward higher education. I vividly remember my mother once telling me, "Look, my son, you should study as much as you can, no matter how hard it gets. I will always be there to support you." These words have been a constant source of inspiration whenever I felt demotivated.

My father left Nepal for India in search of stable employment to support our family. He spent 35 years in India, holding onto the hope of one day returning home to live happily with us. Unfortunately, that dream was never realized. He passed away, leaving behind two unfulfilled desires that will remain with us forever.

My educational journey began in a local school in Madhuban. For higher education, I moved to a nearby city, Butwal, and later to the capital city, Kathmandu, for my bachelor's studies. During my bachelor's, my maternal cousin brought me a book by Stephen Hawking and said, "Look, you have a sharp mind and are fully healthy. You can achieve so much. Take inspiration from him (Stephen Hawking).

Theory of everything book in Nepali

Despite being physically ill, he is doing great." Those words motivated me deeply, and I started reading multiple books on science and questioning everything around me. Before that, I was a simple person, accepting whatever was there without question, without deep thinking, and without a purpose in life. That moment made me realize I needed to move further. To be good and do good, you need to place yourself in a supportive environment that helps you grow. I decided to pursue academic research. That’s why I came to Germany for my master’s in computer science at the University of Bonn, with a dream to do something similar to what Stephen Hawking achieved. But it was not easy to study in a foreign land. Those years were incredibly challenging for me.

My struggles continued, and then something happened that hit me deeply—I lost my father. He was my inspiration, someone I admired greatly, someone who was so close to me, and most importantly, my backbone. It was the hardest part of my life.

My father at his work, India

</div> Amid the turbulence of suffering, I started asking myself profound questions: "Who am I?" Previously, I had been asking questions like, "What is out there? What are the laws governing physical reality?" But now, my focus shifted to deeper philosophical questions: "Who am I? What is it that makes me, me? What is consciousness? Why am I here? Why does anything exist at all?" My naturally curious nature pushed me deeper into spiritual and philosophical areas. I studied many spiritual and philosophical books and started meditating daily. Sometimes, I would sit for entire days, focusing on these deep questions, asking myself repeatedly. I thought, maybe this is my direction, my purpose in this world. At one point, I even considered joining a spiritual institution and leaving the world behind.

But then, I started questioning again: "Why this and not that? Since I have a deep desire for science, why should I leave it?" While all this was happening internally, I was not performing at my best in my studies. I completed a couple of research internships during this period of internal disturbance, but I was unsure of what I wanted to do with my life. My internal research was in full swing. After completing a few research internships at the Max Planck Institute in Tübingen, I decided that maybe I needed to do more research on consciousness. However, I realized I lacked formal knowledge in this direction. I joined the Cognitive Science program in Kaiserslautern, hoping to learn more and conduct research on consciousness. But the program did not meet my expectations, and my frustration grew. I had immense curiosity and a desire to do something meaningful, but I couldn’t find the right mentor or environment. I lost hope and motivation, which led me to quit the program and start working as a Data Scientist at CIMPA GmbH.

I have been working here for two and a half years, but the child inside me kept saying, "You need to do research. You are meant for research." Slowly, I started exploring in my free time. That’s when I encountered quantum computing, which reignited my inner curiosity. I started watching lectures, reading books, and attending events like workshops, hackathons, and seminars to explore my interests further. I found it incredibly exciting and had an "aha" moment—this is what I want to do in my life. Quantum computing is the perfect blend of all my interests: physics, computation, philosophy, and mathematics. This is the direction I want to pursue, where I can fulfill my thirst for knowledge and contribute something meaningful to the world before I leave it.

Besides my intellectual pursuits, I have always had a deep passion for cycling, a hobby that has been with me since childhood. Cycling has been a constant companion throughout my life, from my early days as a 10-year-old enthusiast riding through my village to participating in events like the Cyclassic Race in 2023.

Over the years, my interests have evolved beyond cycling. I have ventured into triathlons, which combine swimming, cycling, and running, pushing me to new physical and mental limits. Additionally, I have developed a passion for climbing. These activities not only keep me active but also serve as a way to reconnect with myself and find balance in life.

Now, I can say that clarity comes through struggles and curiosity about those struggles. Whoever I am today, and my understanding of myself, has evolved through the sacrifices and encouragement of my family and friends, my struggles, and my relentless curiosity. I carry their dreams with me as I continue this journey.
