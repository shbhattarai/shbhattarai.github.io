Course Overview

This advanced course explores the fundamental possibilities and limitations of quantum information processing, examining how information theory perspectives can illuminate theoretical physics. The course provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for understanding quantum computation and quantum information science.

Core Topics Covered

Quantum Entanglement and Correlations

  • EPR paradox and Bell inequalities
  • Entanglement as a resource for quantum information processing
  • Quantum vs. classical correlations
  • Non-locality and its implications

Quantum State Characterization

  • Tools for measuring nearness of quantum states
  • Fidelity and trace distance measures
  • Quantum state discrimination
  • Tomography and state estimation

Quantum Operations and Channels

  • Characterizing the most general possible quantum operations
  • Completely positive maps and Kraus representations
  • Quantum channels and their properties
  • Decoherence and noise models

Information Measures in Quantum Systems

  • Von Neumann entropy and quantum information
  • Mutual information and conditional entropy
  • Relative entropy and quantum divergences
  • Entropic uncertainty relations

Stabilizer Formalism

  • Pauli operators and stabilizer groups
  • Stabilizer states and Clifford operations
  • Gottesman-Knill theorem
  • Applications to quantum error correction

Quantum Error-Correcting Codes

  • Classical error correction principles
  • Quantum error correction basics
  • CSS codes and stabilizer codes
  • Fault-tolerant quantum computation

Theory of Quantum Computation

  • Quantum Turing machines and computational models
  • Universality and gate sets
  • Quantum circuit complexity
  • Measurement-based quantum computation

Quantum Algorithms

  • Deutsch-Jozsa and Simon’s algorithms
  • Grover’s search algorithm
  • Shor’s factoring algorithm
  • Quantum simulation methods

Complexity Theory

  • Classical complexity classes (P, NP, BQP)
  • Quantum complexity classes
  • Quantum advantage and supremacy
  • Lower bounds and impossibility results

Course Structure

Lectures and Problem Sets

  • Weekly lectures covering theoretical foundations
  • Extensive problem sets focusing on mathematical derivations
  • Computational exercises using Qiskit framework
  • Research paper discussions

Research Components

  • Literature review of current quantum information research
  • Implementation of basic quantum algorithms
  • Analysis of quantum error correction schemes
  • Exploration of open problems in the field

Learning Outcomes

Upon completion of this course, students will:

  • Understand the fundamental limits of quantum information processing
  • Master mathematical tools for quantum state analysis
  • Comprehend quantum error correction principles
  • Gain insight into quantum computational complexity
  • Be prepared for advanced research in quantum information science

Instructors

  • Alex May: Expert in quantum information theory and quantum foundations
  • Bindiya Arora: Specialist in quantum algorithms and complexity theory
